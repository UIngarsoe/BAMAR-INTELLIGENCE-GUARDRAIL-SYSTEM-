## Opening Question

The teacher enters and asks first:

> “Students — when a piece of data arrives, may we immediately call it information?”

Student: “Not yet, teacher.”

Teacher: “Good. Then how do we test reliability if we do not even know what type of data it is?”

Today’s TAIE-4 lesson begins there.

---

## 1. DATA ≠ INFORMATION ≠ KNOWLEDGE

```
DATA → INFORMATION → INTERPRETATION → KNOWLEDGE → DECISION
```

| Stage | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Data** | Received facts, records, observations |
| **Information** | Data given context and meaning |
| **Interpretation** | What the information might imply |
| **Knowledge** | Understanding tested and refined over time |
| **Decision** | Action grounded in that understanding |

**Most important:** Possession of data does not equal possession of truth.

---

## 2. First Data Map — Eight Families

| Data Type | Simple description | Intelligence strength |
|-----------|--------------------|------------------------|
| **Structured** | Organised in tables | Easy comparison & calculation |
| **Unstructured** | Images, audio, text, video | Rich context |
| **Semi-Structured** | Tags/fields, not full tables | Machine-friendly bridge |
| **OSINT** | Publicly available sources | Cross-checkable |
| **HUMINT** | Human observation / report | Ground context |
| **SIGINT / Technical** | Electronic / technical signals | Technical patterns |
| **Big Data** | Very large volume / variety | Scale & pattern detection |
| **Small Data** | Small but close observation | Micro-reality insight |

Data type is **not** a reliability score.  
OSINT, HUMINT, Big Data, and Small Data are never automatically true.

---

## 3. Structured Data

Example: Date | Price | Location tables. Machines love them.

Teacher asks: “Price has become 125. Does that prove people have become poorer?”

Not yet.

**Metric ≠ Meaning.**  
A number is not yet a story.

---

## 4. Unstructured Data

Social posts, photos, video, voice messages, spoken words — often without predefined tables.

Absence of structure does not equal absence of value.  
Sometimes unstructured data holds the richest context.  
Machines may struggle; humans may still extract meaning.

---

## 5. Semi-Structured Data

JSON, XML, log files, metadata-rich email.  
Not full tables, not pure chaos.  
A bridge between machine and human.

Think of structure as a continuum:

```
Structured ↔ Semi-Structured ↔ Unstructured
```

---

## 6. OSINT — What Everyone Can See

Public news, academic publications, official statements, public databases, web archives.

**Public ≠ True.**

Public availability proves only that the item is publicly available. It does not automatically prove truth.

Analyst questions:

- Who released it?
- When?
- Where is the original?
- Do independent sources corroborate?
- Are alternative explanations available?

---

## 7. HUMINT — What a Human Saw First

Directly connected to TAIE-3.  
A person on the ground may say: “Conditions here are changing.”  
That can be a HUMINT signal.

**HUMINT ≠ automatically verified fact.**

Memory fails. Perception errs. Bias exists. Deception is possible.

**TAIE formula:**

```
Human Signal + Provenance + Corroboration + Uncertainty
```

---

## 8. SIGINT / Technical Data

Electronic transmissions, telemetry, digital logs, network data. Often highly precise.

**Technical precision ≠ contextual completeness.**

A log may show *what* happened; it may not show *why*.  
Always reconnect technical data to context.

---

## 9. Big Data — Volume Is Not Wisdom

Volume · Velocity · Variety.  
AI can process scale and find correlations.

Does more data always bring us closer to truth? **No.**

Large volumes of garbage remain large garbage.

**Big Data ≠ Big Wisdom.**

---

## 10. Small Data — Today’s Critical Section

A field note. A resident’s observation. A shop’s daily condition. A family’s lived experience.  
Quantity may be one; context may be one hundred.

This returns us to **Reality Proximity** (TAIE-3).

Small Data does not prove population-level truth.  
It can be an invaluable signal for: *What is happening here, now?*

---

## 11. Core Intelligence Drill

**Scenario:** Someone in a locality says, “Movement conditions today are not normal.”

Do not answer True or False. Work the steps:

| Step | Question |
|------|----------|
| 1 | Data type? → HUMINT / Small Data |
| 2 | Source proximity? → Near the event? |
| 3 | Observation or interpretation? → “No cars” vs “Road blocked” |
| 4 | Evidence? → Photo? Other witnesses? |
| 5 | Alternative explanations? → Rain, breakdown, other causes? |
| 6 | Confidence? → Emerging Signal |
| 7 | Next verification? → Independent sources |

When students can do this, they have moved from *reading* data to *thinking with* data.

---

## 12. Data Fusion — Do Not Rely on One Stream

| Stream | Contribution |
|--------|--------------|
| **HUMINT** | “Conditions are changing.” |
| **OSINT** | Similar public reports appear. |
| **Technical Data** | Supporting digital indicators. |
| **Small Data** | Local context. |
| **Big Data** | Wider pattern check. |

**Combined path:**

```
Signal → Corroboration → Context → Calibrated Assessment
```

---

## 13. Sentinel Data Rule

> **Classify the data before you assign confidence.**

Ask about:

- Source  
- Structure  
- Proximity  
- Evidence  
- Context  
- Unknown  

---

## 14. Śīla · Samādhi · Paññā — Data Ethics

| Foundation | Application to Data |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Śīla** | Do not deliberately falsify data. |
| **Samādhi** | Do not rush to judgment on incomplete data. |
| **Paññā** | Separate data, evidence, interpretation, and conclusion. |

Handled this way, data work itself becomes an ethical practice.

## 16. TAIE-4 Final Sentinel Formula

```
DATA TYPE → SOURCE → PROXIMITY → PROVENANCE → EVIDENCE → CONTEXT → CORROBORATION → UNCERTAINTY → JUDGMENT
```
