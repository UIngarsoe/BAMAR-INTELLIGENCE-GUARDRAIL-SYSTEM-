 # 🦚🧘‍♂️🤺 SSISM INTEL — SENTINEL INTELLIGENCE JUDGMENT RULES

## Rule 1 — CLAIM SIZE ≤ EVIDENCE SIZE

This is the foundation.

### > The larger the claim, the larger and stronger the evidence required.



A small observation can support a small claim.

One person's statement cannot automatically support a claim about an entire generation.

One detected pattern cannot prove misconduct.

One event cannot prove a national trend.

Sentinel principle:

Claim > Evidence → Overclaiming

Claim ≤ Evidence → Calibrated claim


---

## Rule 2 — CONFIDENCE ≤ EVIDENCE CAPACITY

This is the rule that follows Rule 1.

> You cannot have greater confidence than the evidence is capable of supporting.



For example:

Evidence: one interview
→ Confidence in one person's opinion: potentially reasonable.

But:

Evidence: one interview
→ Confidence that an entire generation believes the same thing: very low.

So:

Confidence is constrained by Evidence Capacity.


---

## Rule 3 — SOURCE COUNT ≠ EVIDENCE COUNT

Ten websites repeating the same original statement are not necessarily ten independent pieces of evidence.

Therefore:

> 10 repetitions of 1 source ≠ 10 independent confirmations.



SSISM asks:

Who originated the information?

Are the sources independent?

Did they actually observe the event?


---

## Rule 4 — PUBLIC ≠ TRUE

Something being:

on television,

on Facebook,

on YouTube,

widely shared,

officially announced,


does not automatically make it true.

Therefore:

Visibility ≠ Veracity


---

## Rule 5 — OBSERVATION ≠ INTERPRETATION

Example:

> “A Gen-Z speaker said AI will not produce a positive future for America.”



That is an observation.

Interpretation:

> “Gen Z opposes AI.”



That is an inference.

The second statement requires additional evidence.


---

## Rule 6 — PATTERN ≠ INTENT

A detected pattern can tell us:

> Something appears unusual.



It cannot automatically tell us:

> Why someone did it.



Therefore:

Pattern → investigate

not:

Pattern → accuse

This became especially important in our TAIE-9 mathematical-intelligence teaching.


---

## Rule 7 — DETECTION ≠ PROOF

A detector, score, flag, classifier or AI system can produce a signal.

But:

> Signal ≠ Proof



and:

> Proof ≠ Intent



and:

> Intent ≠ Misconduct



Each requires its own evidence.


---

## Rule 8 — METRIC ≠ MEANING

This is one of our strongest rules.

A number measures something.

It does not necessarily tell us what that thing means.

Examples:

Likes ≠ Love

Views ≠ Value

Score ≠ Intelligence

AI detector probability ≠ Human misconduct

Economic loss ≠ Technology failure

Usage ≠ Trust


---

## Rule 9 — POSSIBILITY ≠ PROBABILITY ≠ CERTAINTY

This is critical.

Something can be:

Possible

It could happen.

Probable

Evidence makes it more likely.

Certain

Evidence establishes it to the required standard.

Therefore:

> “Could happen” is not “will happen.”



And:

> “Likely” is not “proven.”




---

## Rule 10 — CONTRADICTION ≠ CAUSATION

Two things may appear inconsistent without one causing the other.

Our AI example is excellent:

> Gen Z uses AI heavily.



and

> Some Gen Z people distrust AI.



That is not necessarily a contradiction.

Possible explanation:

High exposure → greater familiarity → greater awareness of weaknesses → greater criticism.

But that is still a hypothesis requiring evidence.


---

## Rule 11 — ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS MUST BE TESTED

Before accepting the first explanation:

> Generate plausible alternatives.



For example:

“Young people criticize AI.”

Possible explanations:

economic anxiety;

employment concerns;

authenticity concerns;

privacy concerns;

environmental concerns;

political distrust;

personal experience;

normal technological skepticism;

dissatisfaction with particular AI companies.


The intelligence analyst doesn't select the favorite explanation first.


---

## Rule 12 — UNKNOWN IS A VALID INTELLIGENCE OUTPUT

This is very important to SSISM.

Sometimes the correct answer is:

> “We don't know yet.”



That is not failure.

It is better than manufacturing certainty.

Therefore:

Unknown ≠ Ignorance

Unknown = Evidence boundary


---

## Rule 13 — CONTEXT CHANGES MEANING

The same information can mean different things depending upon:

who said it;

where;

when;

to whom;

under what role;

for what purpose;

with what audience.


This connects directly to our Cowboy's Hat lesson.

> Know which hat you are wearing before you interpret or transmit information.




---

## Rule 14 — HUMAN EXPERIENCE ≠ MACHINE MEASUREMENT

A machine may measure:

words;

patterns;

probabilities;

similarity;

behavior;

numerical signals.


But measurement does not automatically capture:

intention;

lived experience;

meaning;

motivation;

moral responsibility.


Therefore:

> Measurement informs judgment; measurement does not automatically become judgment.




---

## Rule 15 — CONSEQUENCE PROPORTIONALITY

This should become one of the formal SSISM judgment rules.

> The greater the consequence of a judgment, the stronger the required evidence and human review.



For example:

A low-confidence observation might be acceptable for:

> “Let's investigate this.”



It is not sufficient by itself for:

> “Punish this person.”



Therefore:

Judgment strength must rise with consequence.


---

## Rule 16 — UPDATE WHEN NEW EVIDENCE ARRIVES

Intelligence is not a one-time declaration.

It is:

Initial evidence → initial judgment → new evidence → reassessment → updated confidence

A good Sentinel is willing to say:

> “Our previous assessment was based on incomplete evidence. The new evidence changes our confidence.”



That is not weakness.

That is intelligence.
