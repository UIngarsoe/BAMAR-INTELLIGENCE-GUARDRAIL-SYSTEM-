## 1. Holmes — Elimination

Holmes would refuse to begin with:

> “Facebook lost in America, therefore other countries can sue Facebook.”

Too large a claim. Break it down.

### Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis |
|----|------------|
| **H1** | A U.S. legal outcome can provide useful evidence for foreign litigation. |
| **H2** | A U.S. outcome automatically establishes Meta’s liability in another country. |
| **H3** | Foreign plaintiffs may have independent legal causes of action against Meta. |
| **H4** | The same underlying conduct may violate different countries’ consumer-protection, privacy, competition, child-safety or negligence laws. |
| **H5** | A foreign court may reject the claim because jurisdiction, causation, limitation periods, applicable law, or evidentiary requirements differ. |

**Eliminate:** H2 is too strong. A U.S. settlement is not automatically a worldwide judgment.  
**H1, H3 and H4** remain plausible and deserve jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction investigation.

> **Holmes question:**  
> What survives after we eliminate everything the American case does **NOT** legally establish?

---

## 2. Columbo — The Missing Questions

Columbo would not ask “Can foreigners sue Facebook?” Too broad.  
He would keep asking: **“Just one more thing…”**

1. What exactly did the U.S. plaintiffs prove or allege?  
   (addictive design / misleading representations / youth mental-health harm / privacy / consumer protection / data collection / product design)

2. Did the alleged conduct also occur in the plaintiff’s country?

3. Which local law protects the person who claims harm?

4. Where did the harm occur?

5. Can the foreign court exercise jurisdiction over Meta?

6. What evidence connects Meta’s conduct to this particular person’s injury?

7. What changed after the American litigation?

---

## 3. Hoover — Institutional Caution

Hoover would look beyond the individual lawsuit:

> “What happens when a global corporation operates one technological system across dozens of legal systems?”

Meta’s conduct may be globally distributed while:

```
law → jurisdiction → regulator → court → remedy
```

remain nationally or regionally divided.

The deeper question:

> Can national legal systems independently regulate a global digital platform?

This is a **global digital-governance** problem, not merely a Facebook problem.

---

## 4. The International Possibility Map

Student 4 should **stop** saying:

> “Facebook lost in America, so everyone else can sue.”

Instead say:

> “The U.S. litigation creates potentially relevant evidence, precedents, factual findings, investigative material, and regulatory momentum that foreign plaintiffs or regulators may examine under their own legal systems.”

That is a far more defensible intelligence claim. Meta’s own disclosures and public reporting already identify significant non-U.S. litigation (UK collective proceedings, European cases including the Spanish damages judgment).

## 5. The New Student 4 Equation

$$
\text{Foreign Litigation Potential} = J \times L \times E \times C \times R
$$

| Variable | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **J** | Jurisdiction — Can the foreign court hear the case? |
| **L** | Local Law — Does local law create a cause of action? |
| **E** | Evidence — Can the plaintiff prove the relevant conduct? |
| **C** | Causation — Can the plaintiff connect the conduct to the injury? |
| **R** | Remedy — Can the court actually provide meaningful relief? |

**Classroom 51 control still applies:**

$$
\text{Claim Size} \leq \text{Evidence Capacity}
$$

- “Meta could potentially face foreign litigation” → reasonable research hypothesis  
- “Meta will definitely lose foreign lawsuits because it lost in America” → unjustifiably large claim
