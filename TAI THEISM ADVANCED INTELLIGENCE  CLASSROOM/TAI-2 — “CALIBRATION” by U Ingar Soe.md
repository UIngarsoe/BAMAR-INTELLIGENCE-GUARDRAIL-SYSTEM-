🕵️ TAI-2 — Holmes

Holmes would ask:

> “What must be true for Thailand's desired outcome to occur?”



Let's construct competing hypotheses.

H1

Thailand's calibrated re-engagement is primarily intended to restore ASEAN engagement and regional stability.

H2

It is partly intended to reduce cross-border security and criminal-economy pressures.

H3

If conditions improve inside Myanmar, Thailand could eventually facilitate more voluntary refugee returns.

H4

Diplomatic engagement alone will produce sufficient security for large-scale sustainable return.

H5

Re-engagement could occur without producing conditions suitable for refugee return.

Now eliminate.

H4 is not established.

Dialogue does not automatically create safety.

H3 remains conditional.

H1 and H2 have strong policy evidence behind them.

Thailand itself says cooperation with Myanmar is needed on narcotics, transnational crime and border problems that neither country can solve alone.

🚬 TAI-2 — Columbo

😂

He would ask Thailand:

1. What exactly does “re-engagement” mean operationally?


2. What measurable result would count as success?


3. Has violence actually decreased?


4. Are refugees returning voluntarily?


5. Can returnees remain safely after returning?


6. Who verifies conditions on the Myanmar side?

8. Is humanitarian access improving?

That last question is critical.

Because returning people and creating conditions in which people voluntarily return are not the same policy.

---

🎯 The “Thai Dream” must therefore be calibrated

We should not write:

> “Thailand's policy is designed to force all Myanmar refugees back.”



That claim exceeds the evidence.

A more defensible hypothesis is:

> Thailand may seek a regional environment in which the causes of cross-border displacement, security threats and humanitarian pressure are reduced sufficiently that more people can eventually return voluntarily and sustainably.



That is much stronger intelligence writing because it separates:

policy objective

from

policy mechanism

from

actual outcome.

📐 The New TAI Calibration Model

I would teach students this:

\[
\boxed{
Return\ Potential =
S \times V \times R \times D
}
\]

Where:

S = Safety

Are conditions sufficiently safe?

V = Voluntariness

Is return genuinely voluntary?

R = Reintegration

Can people actually rebuild their lives?

D = Durability

Will the conditions remain stable?

If any component is weak, the apparent “return success” may be misleading.

And this connects beautifully to Classroom 51:

\[
\boxed{\text{Claim Size} \leq \text{Evidence Capacity}}
\]

So:

> “Thailand is talking to Myanmar” = OBSERVED



> “Thailand wants reduced border pressure” = SUPPORTED POLICY HYPOTHESIS



> “Re-engagement will make Myanmar safe” = UNKNOWN



> “Therefore all refugees will return” = UNSUPPORTED LARGE CLAIM

Voluntary return

Scenario B — Failed calibration

↓

Then re-engagement has not solved the refugee problem.
