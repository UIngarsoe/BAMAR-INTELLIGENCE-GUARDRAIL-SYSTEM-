🪷 Part I – Buddha's Teaching

Pāli

> Ekāyano ayaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṃ visuddhiyā... yadidaṃ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā.



Burmese

"ရဟန်းတို့... ဤသတိပဋ္ဌာန် လေးပါးသည် စိတ်သန့်ရှင်းရေးအတွက် တစ်ခုတည်းသော လမ်းကြောင်းဖြစ်၏။"

Meaning

The teaching presents mindfulness as a path for cultivating ethical awareness, concentration, and insight through sustained observation.


---

🪷 Part II – Mahasi Sayadaw

Mahasi Sayadaw explained that Vicikicchā (ဝိစိကိစ္ဆာ) is one of the hindrances that can obstruct meditation practice. Rather than remaining in uncertainty, practitioners are encouraged to investigate experience through careful observation and practice.

This emphasis is consistent with the insight tradition: direct experience plays an important role alongside study and reflection.

🧠 Masterclass Teaching

Today we distinguish three stages of learning:

🌟 Stage 1 – Learning

Read carefully.

Listen carefully.

Ask questions respectfully.


---

🌟 Stage 2 – Observation

Observe experience patiently.

Notice change.

Avoid rushing to conclusions.


---

🌟 Stage 3 – Wisdom

Reflect on what has been observed.

Compare with reliable teachings and evidence.

Act ethically.


---

🌿 Practical Exercise

Spend several minutes observing:

Walking

Standing

Sitting

Breathing


Notice:

> arising...



> changing...



> passing...



without immediately judging the experience.


---

🌍 Civic Intelligence Reflection

A useful principle in education, research, and civic life is to distinguish between:

assumptions,

interpretations,

and observations supported by evidence.


Developing the habit of careful observation can improve both meditation practice and analytical thinking.


---

🌸 Community Reflection

Ask yourself:

> "Am I reacting immediately?"



or

> "Have I taken time to observe carefully before deciding?"



Thoughtful learning grows from patience, investigation, and ethical conduct.
